# Synthetic catchment dummy data - attribute scale & distribution reasoning

100 fabricated locations, generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic_catchment_dummy_data.py`. **Not real data** - do not merge into `data/location_features/*.json`. Every attribute is drawn conditional on a per-location `urbanicity` class so the dataset has realistic internal correlation (e.g. dense catchments also skew younger and better-served by transit), not 25 independent columns.

| Category | Attribute | Scale | Distribution | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Socio-demographic | `urbanicity` | Nominal | Categorical (3-class multinomial draw) | A location label (urban core/suburban/rural), not a measured quantity. Treated as nominal for Gower distance (simple match/mismatch) even though a density-based order exists - this matches how `locality_type`/`locality_type_l2` are already encoded in this project's real Gower pipeline; encode as ordinal instead only if the downstream metric should respect the order. |
| Socio-demographic | `population_density_km2` | Ratio | Log-normal | Density is strictly positive and spans orders of magnitude (rural to dense urban core); log-normal is the standard choice for any strictly-positive, right-skewed physical quantity. |
| Socio-demographic | `population_size` | Ratio | Log-normal | Same reasoning as density: population counts across locations are classically right-skewed (many small towns, few large cities) and strictly positive - log-normal (or Zipf-like at the aggregate level) is the textbook choice. |
| Socio-demographic | `population_growth_pct_yr` | Interval | Normal | A rate that can be negative (real shrinkage, common outside Romania's major cities) with no meaningful ratio interpretation (4% growth is not 'twice' 2% growth in the way population size has true ratios) - interval scale, and growth rates cluster symmetrically around a mean, so Normal is the standard generative choice. |
| Socio-demographic | `working_age_pct / elderly_pct / families_with_kids_pct` | Ratio (proportion) | Beta (mean/concentration parameterization) | Each is a bounded [0,1] proportion; Beta is the canonical distribution for a bounded proportion, more flexible than a truncated Normal and always valid for any positive concentration parameter. |
| Socio-demographic | `household_income_median_ron` | Ratio | Log-normal | Income is THE textbook log-normal/Pareto-tailed example: strictly positive, heavily right-skewed by a small number of high-income households. |
| Socio-demographic | `household_income_p10/p90_ron` | Ratio | Derived quantiles of a per-location log-normal | Rather than draw independent deciles, each location's own within-catchment income dispersion (`income_dispersion_sigma`, itself Gamma-distributed - a scale parameter must be positive and is itself typically right-skewed) parameterizes a log-normal, and P10/P90 are read off as its quantiles. This keeps the median/decile relationship internally consistent, not two unrelated draws. |
| Socio-demographic | `household_size_avg` | Ratio | Normal (clipped to a physically plausible range) | Household size varies within a narrow, realistic band (roughly 1.2-5 persons) with mild, roughly-symmetric variation around a central tendency - a clipped Normal is simpler and just as defensible here as a Gamma, since the skew this variable actually exhibits is minor. |
| Socio-demographic | `pct_single_person_households` | Ratio (proportion) | Beta | Bounded proportion - same reasoning as the age-share variables above. |
| Socio-demographic | `pct_education_primary/secondary/tertiary` | Ratio (compositional) | Dirichlet | These three shares must sum to 100% - Dirichlet is the multivariate generalization of Beta for exactly this simplex-valued (compositional) case, and is the correct choice whenever generating a full categorical breakdown rather than one isolated proportion. |
| Socio-demographic | `employment_rate_pct` | Ratio (proportion) | Beta | Bounded proportion of the labor force that is employed. |
| Socio-demographic | `pct_white_collar/blue_collar/other_of_employed` | Ratio (compositional) | Dirichlet | Occupation mix among the employed population sums to 100% by construction - same compositional reasoning as the education shares. |
| Competitive landscape | `competitors_{tier}_count` | Ratio (count) | Negative binomial | Count data (non-negative integers). Poisson is the naive default for counts, but competitor stores cluster spatially (agglomeration in retail districts), producing more variance than Poisson's mean=variance assumption allows - negative binomial's extra dispersion parameter captures that overdispersion. Mean is scaled by each location's population size, since competitor density is not independent of catchment size. |
| Competitive landscape | `distance_nearest_competitor_km` | Ratio | Rayleigh | This is not an arbitrary choice: for points distributed via a 2D spatial Poisson process with intensity lambda, the distance to the nearest neighbor is a known, exact result - Rayleigh distributed (the sqrt-of-two-squared-normals special case of Weibull). Scale parameter is tied inversely to each location's own competitor density for internal consistency. |
| Competitive landscape | `footfall_generators_count` | Ratio (count) | Poisson | Malls/transit hubs are rare, roughly independent large-footprint facilities (typically 0-3 per catchment) - unlike the denser, cluster-prone competitor-store counts above, Poisson's mean=variance assumption is defensible at this low, sparser count range. |
| Competitive landscape | `complementary_retail_ratio` | Ratio (proportion) | Beta | A bounded [0,1] share of nearby retail that is complementary rather than directly competing. |
| Accessibility | `transit_stops_count` | Ratio (count) | Negative binomial | Transit stops cluster along corridors/routes rather than spreading uniformly - overdispersed count data, same reasoning as competitor counts. Mean scales with population density. |
| Accessibility | `transit_avg_headway_min` | Ratio | Gamma | A strictly positive, right-skewed waiting-time-like quantity (most areas get moderate service; a long tail of poorly-served locations pulls the mean up) - Gamma is the standard flexible distribution for positive continuous variables of this shape, and is the theoretically natural counterpart to Poisson-arrival-style processes (headway relates to an inverse arrival rate). |
| Accessibility | `parking_availability` | Ordinal | Binned latent Beta | Modeled as an ordered category (none < limited < moderate < ample) rather than a raw space count, since exact counts are rarely available in practice and it is usually assessed qualitatively - generated via a latent continuous Beta propensity binned into ordered categories, which is the standard generative model behind ordinal (ordered-logit/probit-style) data and preserves its correlation with urbanicity (denser cores have less available parking). |
| Accessibility | `road_avg_commute_speed_kmh` | Ratio | Normal (clipped to a physically plausible range) | Commute speed clusters around a typical value with mild, roughly symmetric variation (congestion pulls it down, open roads pull it up) - a clipped Normal is standard for a physically bounded quantity with a strong central tendency. |
| Accessibility | `foot_traffic_index` | Interval | Normal composite (z-scored inputs + noise, min-max scaled) | This is a derived, normalized index (as real footfall-vendor indices typically are), not a raw physical count - its zero point and units are arbitrary, which is the defining property of an interval (not ratio) scale. Generated as a weighted combination of density, transit, footfall-generator, and competitor z-scores plus Gaussian noise, so it stays internally consistent with the other correlated variables rather than being an unrelated draw. |
